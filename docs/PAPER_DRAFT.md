# No One Saw Everything

*Learning Collective Memory of Hidden Objects*

## Abstract

Robots collaborating on a search can miss different events. In our MuJoCo scenario, SO101 observes a placement attempt, Tank observes the relocation of the containers, and ROSMASTER chooses where to search or requests another observation. We train two causal visual evidence models with the same architecture and compare event supervision with event and terminal-reveal supervision. The reveal stays outside the policy's inputs. On 100 independent source worlds spanning six scenario families, both learned selective policies chose the correct first container in 34 of 34 resolvable container cases and requested inspection on all 32 ambiguous variants at each tested team size. An observation-matched broadcast baseline tied on these endpoints. At eight robots, the event-only selective method used 2,417 mean bytes per case and the centralized RGB reference used 547,717 compressed input bytes. On 16 independent matched ambiguity pairs, changing hidden evaluator truth preserved the complete permitted input and the coupled prediction. The experiment did not establish an accuracy or calibration benefit from terminal-reveal supervision. These results establish the simulated search and uncertainty behavior. Physical validation and the learned motor comparison remain open. [Independent test results](PRIMARY_RESULTS_INDEPENDENT.md)

## Research question and hypotheses

Can three robots choose where to search for a hidden object when no robot witnessed all the events? We assume the placement may have failed and containers may move or change contents. A useful policy should answer when the histories resolve the location and request inspection when they leave it uncertain.

1. Combining timed private placement and relocation evidence improves correct search over either witness individually, at matched observation and communication budgets.
2. Terminal-reveal supervision improves a small causal evidence model's probability estimates and selective accuracy when its visual observations are usable.
3. An observationally matched hidden-truth pair induces the same pre-inspection prediction with coupled randomness. A resolvable control distinguishes a useful abstention rule from a policy that always requests inspection.

## Method and dataset

SO101 witnesses a placement attempt with wrist and top RGB. Tank acquires front RGB during changes in the exterior relation. ROSMASTER receives allowed, time-stamped factors and chooses a search region, reports an exposed surface or requests inspection. Each source history has assigned capture and delivery boundaries. The permitted packets, messages, state, caches and timing are fixed for the F6 invariance checks. Evaluator truth and terminal reveals are stored separately and may only enter training and scoring.

The implemented visual backbone is frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 with local `resnet18-f37072fd.pth` weights. The witness heads are two 48-unit GRUs and readout layers with 235,927 trainable parameters in total. The arm head predicts an observed settled placement class and the Tank head predicts the occupied identity permutation. Categorical visual scores feed a log-space update over ten joint states. A validation-selected threshold determines whether the maximum-probability answer is disclosed. The event-only and event-plus-reveal variants share architecture, admitted inputs and splits. Unobserved intermediate labels are masked.

The shared MuJoCo experiment uses the existing SO101, Tank and ROSMASTER models. Families cover success and relocation, failed placement, contents change, ambiguous identity across a crossing or occlusion, nesting and withheld decisive evidence. Independent scene groups, including descendants and matched variants, stay together. The bounded first computer study targets 80/20 motor trajectories with 20 fresh starts and 300/60/60 story groups. Expansion gates authorize up to 1,000/200 motor trajectories and 5,000/1,000 story histories after generation quality is established. The physical plan reserves four attended blocks of six stories and aims eventually for about 200/40 motor and 160/40 visual training and validation sources, followed by a fresh separately frozen cohort. ## Predeclared pilot analysis before frozen holdout scoring

The primary measure is the first correct container choice among resolvable left/right cases, counting inspection as a missed first choice. Surface decisions, F6 inspection, confidently wrong choices, Brier score and negative log likelihood remain separately interpretable measures. Report paired independent-group bootstrap intervals for the exact learned filter versus the calibrated RGB particle filter and versus the particle filter with identical learned likelihoods.
